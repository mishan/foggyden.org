PluralWare privacy policy

PluralWare is a phone and Wear OS app for PluralKit systems, made by Misha Nasledov. It has no accounts, ads or analytics, and sends nothing to its developer.

What it handles, and where it goes

  • Your PluralKit token is stored encrypted on your phone, and on your watch if you have one. It goes from your phone to your watch and to PluralKit, and nowhere else.
  • Your system's data (members, switches) is read from and written to PluralKit, at your request, over HTTPS. The apps keep a little of it on the device to show it quickly: the members list, and the current fronters for the widget, complication and tile.
  • Friend notifications, if you share switches, name only the members you choose; anyone else in front shows as "someone". Sharing tells friends who's fronting even if your front is private in PluralKit. Only the friends you add hear anything.
    • Private notifications are end-to-end encrypted. The push service that delivers them can't read them.
    • Simple (ntfy) notifications can be read by whoever runs the ntfy server.
    • Discord channel posts are read by Discord and by everyone in the channel, and they stay there. The webhook link is stored encrypted on your devices.
  • A relay, if you host one, holds your friends' follow codes, any Discord webhooks you added, and the names of the members you chose to share. It never holds your PluralKit token. It runs on a server you control, and it writes the notifications it sends, so whoever runs it can read them, Private ones included.
  • Following friends uses UnifiedPush and the distributor app you choose (such as ntfy).

Nothing is backed up or transferred to a new device: the encryption key never leaves the device. Disconnecting in the app's settings deletes your token and sharing settings from the phone. The watch deletes its copies when it next syncs with the phone. The relay is told to delete its copy; if it can't be reached, the app keeps its address and admin secret, and tries again each time you open sharing settings, until it succeeds or you tell it to forget the relay. Uninstalling deletes everything the app stored on the device.
